Where the ice has left its mark

Over many millennia, the glacier has moved back and forth in rhythm with the ice ages. This has carved down the mountainsides and created beautiful and unique nature

History

The last ice age was a period between 117,000 and 11,700 years ago. During this time, there was an ice sheet up to 3,000 meters thick that covered all of Norway. 10,000 years ago, all the ice had melted. Today's glaciers are therefore not from the last ice age.

Even though the ice was gone, this did not happen without leaving traces. The entire reason that Oldedalen exists is because stones and other material attached themselves under the ice and functioned as a giant sandpaper that carved down the mountain little by little while the ice moved back and forth.

The glacier that is there today took shape about 2,500 years ago. Since then it has grown and melted, but the largest it has been in recent times was during "the little ice age" 300 years ago. After this, it has retreated dramatically. Between 1930 and 1950, the glacier front retreated by a full 800 meters.

Even though the glacier is much smaller than it used to be, it is absolutely still worth the trip. On the hike up you encounter large waterfalls, giant's kettles and other remains from the ice age that was here hundreds of thousands of years ago. At the top you are met by the glacier surrounded by steep, smooth mountainsides.

The glacier as part of something larger

Briksdalsbreen is a smaller part of Jostedalsbreen, which is the largest glacier on the European mainland. The area is around 10 square kilometers. 88 percent of this lies on the ice plateau above 1,500 meters altitude, with some outlets like Briksdalsbreen.

Tourist destination

Ever since the 1800s, Briksdalen has been a popular travel destination. Until 2004, there were horse carriages to the glacier, but in 2004 it switched to unique "troll cars" which are open cars with room for the whole family.
