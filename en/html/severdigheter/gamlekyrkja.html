Olden Old Church

Church on the site for 700 years

Olden Old Church was completed in 1759 and consecrated the same year. The architect is unknown.

History

Olden's location means that strong winds can often occur. This also happened in 1757. Only 11 years earlier, the church owner Peder Pedersen Tonning had torn down the old stave church and built a new long church. The church was traditionally oriented east to west. When the strong south wind hit the church walls, they collapsed. It is told that two church builders had competed for the contract in 1746, and that the one who got the job had boasted about how solid the church was and how long it would stand.

When the church was to be rebuilt, they chose to build it as a compact cross church with a ground plan like a Greek cross with all arms equally long. Like the previous church it was oriented east-west, but the shape made it more resistant to wind. The roof rider (bell tower) is low and compact to prevent wind damage. These measures worked, and today the church stands as the oldest in Inner Nordfjord.

Inventory

Inside the church you are met by bare woodwork. The central aisle has sturdy wooden floors and on both sides there are spacious benches with closed doors. Many of the benches are older than the church itself. This can be seen from the details carved into the doors. Some consist of the owner's initials, some have beautiful decorations, while others contain the year they were made. The oldest of these is dated 1662.

An interesting detail to notice is that on the south side (to the right) the benches have so-called hat hooks - branches cut from young birch for men to hang their hats on. This is not found on the north side. This is because men and women sat segregated when the church was built.

The painted barrel-shaped pulpit is younger than the church itself, probably from the first half of the 1800s. Apart from this, only the altarpiece and altar rail are painted. The altarpiece is painted with the year 1772 and the motif is a communion scene, flanked by Moses and John. Above the main motif we find the crucifixion. At the bottom of the panel we find the text "The cup of blessing which we bless, is it not the communion of the blood of Christ, and the bread which we break, is it not the communion of the body of Christ?"

The Spanish Madonna figure in the northern cross arm was given to the church in the 1940s by the wife of artist William Singer.
