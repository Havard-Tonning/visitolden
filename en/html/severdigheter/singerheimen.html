Singerheimen

Art, culture and history

Singerheimen was built in 1921 by the couple William H. Singer Jr. and Anna Brugh Singer. Today Singerheimen is preserved through use and the hosts offer guided tours, accommodation, quizzes, concerts and afternoon tea.

History

William Singer was born in Pittsburgh in 1868. He inherited a large fortune from the steel company Singer Nimick & Co, but he chose art over business. William married Anna Spencer Brugh in 1895. The couple first visited Norway in 1903 and Olden in 1913. They liked it here, and immediately after World War I they began planning to make their home in Olden. The studio was built in 1914 and the rest of the buildings were erected in 1921. They moved in during the summer of the same year. Singer drew much inspiration from the nature in the area for his paintings.

Impact on the village

Mrs. Singer said she had wishes to buy the entire valley. They never actually did this, but they left deep marks on the poor village. The construction itself provided work for dozens of people, and at most 30 servants worked on the property. The workers often spoke about how caring and warm Anna and William were. In addition to Singerheimen, or Dalheim as it was called at the time, Singer helped with funds to build the road between Olden and Innvik, Nordfjordeid hospital, Olden's new church, the community center and a power plant for the village.

The buildings

The main house at Singerheimen is a villa of almost 1000 square meters. In addition to this there is a cowshed, barn, guest house, wood shed, servant's residence, storehouse and studio. There is also a mausoleum where both William (died 1943) and Anna (died 1962) lie embalmed.
