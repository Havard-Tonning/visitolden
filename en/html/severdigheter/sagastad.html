Interactivity and storytelling

At Nordfjordeid, burial mounds have been discovered several times, showing that there was a center of power here during the Viking Age. Sagastad conveys this history in an interactive way, where the main attraction is the 30-meter long reconstruction of a Viking ship found here.

The burial mounds

There were approximately 200 years of burial mound construction at Myklebust. Being buried in a mound was a custom often reserved for rich and powerful people. The oldest burial mound has been dated to the 700s, while the youngest is from the 900s.
Several objects that were part of the burial have been found in the burial mounds, including swords, axes, an anvil, a hammer, jewelry and kitchen utensils.

The Myklebust Ship

The most important find made in these mounds is a collection of 700 nails. The number of nails, together with other finds, has made it possible to reconstruct the ship. The original was burned as part of the sacrificial ritual, but in 2016 construction of the new Viking ship began. In 2019 the ship was completed and launched for the first time. Not only is it a beautiful ship, but it sits well in the water with good maneuvering characteristics.

VR

At Sagastad, past and future meet. At the center you can put on VR glasses and see how Nordfjordeid may have looked during the Viking Age. You can also try virtual archery.
